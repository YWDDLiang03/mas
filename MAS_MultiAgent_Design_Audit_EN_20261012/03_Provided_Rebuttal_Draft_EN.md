## Original text

We appreciate this important concern and agree that our original CDES analysis did not sufficiently document how the screening criteria were established, challenged, and validated, nor how sensitive the final prioritization was to the adopted rules and thresholds. To address this limitation, we implemented an iterative, tool-augmented multi-agent system. Rather than treating the original CQA criteria as fixed inputs, the framework treats screening rules as revisable scientific hypotheses. Role-specialized agents synthesize target-level, pharmacological, and counter-evidence to propose candidate CQA criteria, including their definitions, directions, thresholds, units, and applicability conditions. Critic and arbiter agents then identify weakly supported, redundant, or conflicting criteria and propose additions, modifications, mergers, or removals. Human experts review these proposed rule updates before they are incorporated into the next iteration. Through successive evidence–critique–human review cycles, the rule set is progressively refined toward the final 11 CQA criteria used for candidate prioritization.

The resulting CQA rule set is then applied to prioritize candidate compounds, after which the agents can invoke the original CDES pipeline, as well as other task-specific computational tools where appropriate, for further quantitative screening and verification. Importantly, the LLM agents do not replace the underlying computational models: they determine which evidence, rules, candidates, and alternative configurations should be examined, whereas CDES and the corresponding computational tools execute the numerical evaluation. Their outputs—including target/CQA scores, rule violations, candidate exclusions, and ranking changes—are returned to the agents as structured feedback. The agents use these results to identify unsupported exclusions, boundary cases, failed rules, and evidence conflicts, which can trigger another round of rule revision and human review. This creates an auditable closed loop of evidence synthesis, rule evolution, candidate prioritization, computational validation, and expert confirmation.

We further used this framework to evaluate the sensitivity of the resulting screening procedure. Selected target and CQA thresholds were systematically varied within prespecified biologically plausible ranges, and the computational screening was rerun under each configuration. Surufatinib remains the top-prioritized compound under the original prespecified configuration, whereas sufficiently large changes to several key criteria alter the ordering of competing candidates. We therefore now explicitly characterize the final CDES-based prioritization as conditional on the adopted rule set and parameter configuration, rather than as a parameter-invariant ranking. The revised manuscript additionally provides the evidence and rule-evolution records for the five TME targets and the final 11 CQA criteria, together with the corresponding candidate-level decisions and computational outputs.
