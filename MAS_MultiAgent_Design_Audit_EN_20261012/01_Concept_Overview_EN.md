# An Agent-Centered, Self-Improving Multi-Agent Platform

Concept with drug prioritization as the first application case | Proposed work | Revised 12 October 2026: agent-first architecture only, visible iteration history, flexible inputs, persistent leaderboard, adaptive continuation, CDES optional

The proposed contribution is an agent-centered system that understands objectives, decomposes and delegates tasks, coordinates proposal and criticism, uses tools, manages evidence and memory, and evaluates changes to its own working strategies. It maintains evidence-backed candidate leaderboards and a traceable history of execution and decisions. Drug prioritization supplies a realistic initial application; the main research and design contribution lies in the reusable agent mechanisms, their evaluation, and their observable behavior.

The concrete v1 purpose is to carry out the work promised in the manuscript rebuttal and substantiate surufatinib's high-priority position under the stated objective and target configuration. The framework is the means to that end. Its design must connect the agent capabilities to actual rule/evidence records, expert review, tool feedback, candidate comparisons, and future sensitivity evaluations. Reusability should not dilute the primary case into an optional demonstration or an abstract platform exercise.

The ovarian-cancer/TME manuscript, its targets, and surufatinib form the required first worked case. User objectives, desired properties, evidence/data, candidate sets, seeds, and constraints should still be accepted in flexible combinations. Surufatinib is a disclosed, already-known reference outcome that the case seeks to substantiate as a leading candidate; it must not become a hard-coded answer for unrelated tasks. Agent capability and scientific support for this particular prioritization require distinct evaluation. Deep biological mechanism analysis, clinical judgments, de novo target discovery, and new wet-lab studies are outside the current architecture assignment.

**Three architectural layers.** The reusable core owns planning, delegation, collaboration, execution state, evidence/provenance, memory, policy versions, progress, human review, and the leaderboard lifecycle. A replaceable domain adapter supplies specialized vocabularies, criteria, datasets, tools, and expert-validation requirements. A case configuration supplies the actual objective, candidate scope, constraints, and available evidence. This separation lets the current biology example exercise the architecture without defining its internals.

**Starting point and division of responsibility.** The MAS receives the scientific objective, relevant literature, the candidate universe, and available computational tools. The manuscript's existing TME hypothesis and targets may supply the initial biological scope. Agents may suggest refinements where evidence warrants them, but de novo target discovery is optional. CDES is optional. The core workflow must support evidence investigation, rule development, and candidate comparison without it. Agents decide what needs examination; available measurements, executable rules, and appropriate numerical tools provide decision inputs. CDES may be connected through an adapter when useful and available.

**Deriving decision criteria.** Agents translate the objective and evidence into proposed attributes and decision rules. In the drug adapter, these attributes are represented as CQAs and may involve target relevance, pharmacology, developability, or safety. The architecture specifies how sources, direction, units, applicability, uncertainty, and proposed thresholds are represented and reviewed. Domain validity remains grounded in appropriate sources, tools, and experts. Missing or uncertain values stay explicit; resolving every biological question is not required to design the workflow.

The agents derive CQAs from the objective and supporting evidence. The final number is not predetermined. The manuscript's original eleven criteria provide historical context for later comparison, rather than a target count for generation. This allows the system to discover that a proposed attribute lacks support, two criteria duplicate one another, an important consideration is missing, or a threshold applies only under particular conditions. These findings should produce explicit additions, revisions, mergers, or removals, each linked to a reason and source.

**Critique and expert review.** A critic challenges unsupported claims, selective evidence use, redundant criteria, incompatible units, and candidate exclusions that depend on weak assumptions. An arbiter reconciles the critique with the proposal, records disagreements, and identifies the next evidence or tool request. Role separation improves the organization of review but does not itself establish independent verification. Where an issue depends on scientific judgment, it is submitted to human experts. Their review decisions are recorded separately from model-generated opinions before proposed changes enter the confirmed screening configuration.

**Connecting decisions to computation.** The approved rule set is applied consistently across candidates using available evidence, curated measurements, an executable rule engine, and suitable optional computational tools. Results return to the agents as structured feedback: target-related evidence or predictions, available CQA measurements or estimates, missing values, violated criteria, exclusions, and ordering changes. An unavailable CDES adapter leaves its potential outputs missing; it does not block the rest of the workflow. Agents investigate boundary cases and contradictions, using the same scrutiny for surufatinib and its competitors. Evidence-only comparisons remain distinguishable from completed numerical validation. LLM-generated confidence cannot substitute for a measurement or an external source.

The intended workflow is:

```text
Research objective and biological scope
    -> evidence and counter-evidence
    -> proposed rules and CQAs
    -> critique, arbitration, and human scientific review
    -> candidate evaluation using available evidence and optional tools
    -> structured results, failures, and boundary cases
    -> revised rules and evidence requests
```

**When to continue.** A single assessment pass may be sufficient when evidence supports a defensible comparison. Several agents can participate in that pass. Another round should address an actionable gap, new external evidence, a conflict, or a targeted computation with decision value. Repetition without new information is not an improvement. The design should compare single-pass, fixed-round, and adaptive policies under consistent tasks and visible budgets, and stop with unresolved status when no useful next step remains. Updating the leaderboard across time is separate from requiring multiple rounds within every run.

**The primary output.** Maintain a versioned leaderboard per comparable task context. Show candidate identity, objective fit, source provenance, measured versus predicted attributes, rule coverage, uncertainty, counter-evidence, review status, and the reason for any position change. Prefer a smaller well-supported shortlist to speculative recommendations; an empty recommended set is legitimate. Keep provisional, unassessed, and rejected entries visible separately. New evidence can demote or remove a candidate as well as promote it. Better justification does not imply monotonically increasing confidence, scores, or list length.

**Visible, reasoned iteration.** The workbench should take Agentic Doping's progress and proposal-review history mechanisms as a concrete design reference. Users should see overall, stage, and round-level progress; the current agent/tool action; waits, retries, and failures; and an inspectable timeline of each analysis. Every substantive decision should link its input/evidence version, relevant sources and tool results, critique, human review where applicable, and concise rationale to the resulting rule, candidate, or leaderboard change. Historical versions remain inspectable. Human edits should identify affected results and required reruns. This provides an evidence trail without requiring raw model responses or private internal reasoning.

**What self-improvement means.** There are two connected forms of revision. Within an investigation, the system improves the rule set and its supporting evidence. Across iterations or runs, it may improve how its agents search for evidence, compare candidates, select tools, and detect recurring errors. Scoped memory retains useful procedures, unresolved gaps, and failed approaches with their provenance. Proposed strategy updates remain provisional until checked; an accepted candidate or an agent's agreement does not automatically become a reusable scientific fact.

Agentic Doping offers a concrete engineering reference for bounded proposal-review cycles, structured tool feedback, evidence records, and provisional methodology reflection. Its inspected implementation does not establish successful self-improvement for this platform. Agent-level evaluation should cover planning/delegation, tool-contract correctness, traceability, memory use, reliable execution and human-edit propagation, precision/coverage, robustness, and cost. Policy comparisons should use fixed evidence snapshots and separate evaluation material. Domain predictive accuracy additionally requires suitable outcome labels; process correctness alone does not demonstrate biological effectiveness. Strong RSI claims should wait for measured results.

The design audit must prioritize the latest primary literature on agent systems, orchestration, memory, tool use, evaluation, RSI, and self-evolving agents at execution time, recording its search cutoff and source versions. It should examine critical findings as well as positive results, including whether extra rounds or agent debate improve decisions after accounting for their cost. Biomedical research should be bounded to what is needed to specify the application adapter. The packaged references provide starting context rather than a completed current survey.

**Testing the nomination.** The proposed study should examine how candidate selection changes under justified alternative rules and thresholds. The sensitivity protocol, parameter ranges, and baseline configuration should be documented before the corresponding evaluation. It should preserve both configurations that retain surufatinib and those that favor alternatives. Ranges selected after inspecting outcomes should be identified as exploratory. This gives the authors a clear account of which assumptions sustain the nomination and which uncertainties remain consequential.

The future system's expected outputs are persistent task-scoped leaderboards, source-linked evidence records, versioned rule/CQA sets, candidate inclusion and exclusion histories, available computational outputs, expert decisions, and evaluations of claimed improvements. The primary case must aim to produce a supported surufatinib prioritization report and the records needed for an accurate rebuttal, including conflicting configurations or unresolved evidence where present. The next agent must specify the architecture, UI state, progress, interfaces, failure handling, rebuttal-to-design mapping, and case protocol. It must not write implementation code or invent the intended ranking. Implementation, scientific evaluations, and genuine expert approvals remain later work.
