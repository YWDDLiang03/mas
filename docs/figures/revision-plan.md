## Figure 1: how the system works

Panel a shows a research objective and source-linked evidence entering a proposer, critic and adjudicator. A labelled feedback arrow returns review findings to the proposer. Evidence and definition checks produce a frozen framework with condition requirements, prioritization criteria and contextual observations. Scored criteria can also carry required-evidence obligations. Unused proposals retain their own branch and reasons. Contextual observations feed interpretation.

Panel b shows task-defined admission assessment. All requirements satisfied leads to Eligible; partial support with no failed condition leads to Evidence incomplete; a required-condition failure or a completed assessment with no admission support leads to Excluded. A confirmed failure takes priority over partial support. The system reports unfinished work separately from final candidate outcomes. Only Eligible enters weighted prioritization. The saved coverage policy determines Ranked / No rank within the eligible group. Framework inputs to admission and scoring are visually identifiable.

The recorded strip within panel a carries the case-specific expansion and refinement trajectory: 0 → 6 task requirements → 1,917 working items → 147 → 68 → 53. Label the last three steps as refinement rounds and show the final partition: 18 enabled decision rules + 13 contextual observations + 22 items not used. ## General medical-research semantics

The task defines the research objects, condition requirements, evidence requirements and scoring comparisons. Admission use and scoring use are independent; a single criterion may have both. Counts use unique rule identities and the scope recorded at the selected freeze. Targets, oral administration and the 1+5 counts are the recorded example. The same relationship applies to carrier properties, sample-type constraints and assay evidence under their own task definitions.
