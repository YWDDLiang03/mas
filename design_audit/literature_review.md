# Literature Review: Agent Systems, Orchestration, Memory, Tool Use, Evaluation, Recursive Self-Improvement, and Self-Evolving Agents

Integrated by the coordinating agent on 12 October 2026 from the two literature workstreams (`agent_reports/current_agent_literature_a.md`, Workstream 6a; `agent_reports/current_agent_literature_b.md`, Workstream 6b) and their cross-reviews. Design-phase document; it records what was read, at what depth, what the sources show, and which design decisions follow. It is not a claim that the survey is exhaustive. Component ids (C1–C20) and vocabularies follow `optimized_design.md` Section 0; decision ids (ADR-nn) follow `architecture_decisions.md`.

## 1. Search cutoff, method, and scope

**Cutoff.** Both investigations executed on 12 October 2026 (UTC); every search, fetch, and status check is dated that day. The priority window was October 2025 to October 2026 plus foundational work (2023–2025 papers on self-correction, correlated errors, preference leakage, AFlow, MAST, the Gödel Agent).

**Tools.** WebSearch (17 dated queries by 6a, 25 by 6b: 42 in total, all listed in the two contribution records) and WebFetch over arXiv abstract pages, arXiv HTML full texts, one arXiv PDF read page by page (AstaBench, pp. 1–20), one PDF extracted locally (DGM v3 appendices E.1 and H), venue pages (neurips.cc, iclr.cc, icml.cc), official GitHub repositories, and one vendor engineering post. 6b additionally grepped the local read-only DGM clone (`reference_repos/dgm`, commit a565fd2d). No code was executed.

**Reading-depth labels.** *Full text (extracted)* / *FT-H*: the complete arXiv HTML was fetched and read through one or more section-targeted extraction passes by a tool-mediated summarizer; quoted numbers are those the passes returned and were cross-checked against the abstract page where both existed; the investigators did not independently re-derive statistics. *Full text (direct)* / *FT-L*: PDF pages read directly. *SEC*: selected sections of a long survey. *Abstract* / *ABS*: arXiv abstract page only. *PAGE*: venue or repository page. *SNIP*: search snippets only. An abstract-level read is never presented as a full-text review anywhere in this document.

**Status verification.** Venue or acceptance is recorded as verified only when a venue page, proceedings link, PDF header, or the arXiv comments field stated it; otherwise "preprint, venue not verified". One secondary-source discrepancy was found and resolved in favour of the primary text (AIDE² reward-hacking rate 55→32 percent in the paper, not 63→34 as news items state).

**Division of scope.** 6a covered agent systems and harnesses, orchestration and planning/delegation, memory and skill acquisition, tool-use and workflow optimization, agent evaluation methodology, and agentic scientific discovery. 6b covered recursive self-improvement and self-evolving agents, agent evolution, and the critical or negative evidence on reflection, debate, extra inference rounds, evaluator and reward exploitation, same-family judging, and generalization. Two bridging sources (Self-Harness; the HAL reasoning-effort finding) appear in 6a without deep analysis. No biomedical self-improvement literature was searched (out of scope by assignment); surveys were read for taxonomy and evaluation sections only.

**Counts.** 6a: 33 sources, 17 at full text. 6b: 35 sources, 18 at full text plus the DGM appendices. Two sources overlap (MAST; ACE), giving 66 unique sources below, 33 of them read at full text by at least one investigator.

## 4. Positive findings

**Orchestration and verification are the decisive failure loci, and structural verification works.** The only large-sample failure taxonomy (L01) places 43.7% of multi-agent failures in specification and design and 24.45% in verification; an orchestrator-attribution study (L02) finds the orchestrator responsible for a mean 67.7% of failures; a vendor post (L03) reports that subagents fail without an objective, output format, tool guidance, and boundaries, and that token usage explains 80% of performance variance. Adding an objective-level verification step gave +15.6% in L01's case study while prompt-only fixes were inconsistent; agents using explicit verification were 13–87% more likely to succeed in L23. This supports typed delegation (the step as the brief, with output contract and boundaries) and typed critique checks (C5, C7).

**External feedback is the one condition under which revision reliably helps.** A one-bit external correctness signal improved every model tested (L57); execution feedback drives the gains in ACE (L07) and AIDE² (L39); harness evolution helped only where tasks were long-horizon with granular verifiable feedback (L42). This is the evidential basis for referent-backed continuation triggers (C8).

**Memory gains are real but conditional.** Itemized, delta-updated memory with helpful/harmful counters beat full-rewrite memory and GEPA on agent tasks (L07: +17.0 vs +4.0 on AppWorld; 75.1% fewer rollouts); failure-derived lessons helped (L08: 46.5→49.7); skills act as procedural anchors (L09). Each gain comes with a lifecycle condition: ACE lost 3.4 points without ground truth; skill-retrieval precision fell from 29.6% to 3.3% as the pool grew from 5 to 100 (L09); cross-role transfer cost 4.8–7.5 points and training gains did not transfer (L10). Human-curated procedures were the only configuration with uniformly positive transfer (L45: 24 of 24 cells).

**Evaluation methodology has matured faster than methods.** ABC, HAL, and AstaBench (L22–L24) converge on cost-controlled, tool-standardized, log-inspected evaluation with trivial-agent baselines and date-cut corpora; τ²-bench adds pass^k reliability over repeated runs (L25); AstaBench shows per-step success near 70% collapsing to about 3% for completing all steps of an end-to-end research task. These are importable requirements for C19.

**Reflective prompt optimization is a credible offline strategy-evolution engine.** GEPA (L15) mutates instructions using a scalar metric plus textual feedback, reaches +9.62% and +12.19% aggregate with up to 35× fewer rollouts than GRPO, and transfers across models; most of its budget is validation, and it requires a well-defined metric. The design adopts it as a candidate for C19/C20, to be evaluated first, never run inside a screening round.

**Claim type and verifier identity matter.** Kosmos's data-derived statements were 85.5% accurate against 57.9% for interpretive syntheses (L28); identical outputs yield 3–18% unsupported-citation rates depending on the verifier, with verifiers agreeing on what is supported but not on what to flag (L29). Hence `claim_type` and `verifier_id` on every ledger claim (C9).

**Promotion mechanics with evidence behind them.** Archives beat replace-only selection because one bad modification poisons a line (L34); a frozen final selector outside the evolvable surface, lower-confidence-bound ranking, and stratified splits touched once are implemented in hsi (L40, confirmed by inspection); staged evaluation budgets and an explicit noise leeway exist in DGM; a version's own score predicts its lineage poorly (L35).

## 5. Critical and negative findings

**Reflection without new information is re-sampling.** Intrinsic self-correction often degrades answers (L58); a second pass yields near-zero information gain on objective tasks and negative gain on subjective ones (L57), and LLM revision degraded even high-quality human answers.

**Homogeneous debate costs more for no gain.** Unguided peer debate consumed 2.1–3.4 times the tokens of isolated self-correction for equal or lower accuracy, with agents adopting the modal answer up to 85.5% of the time and plurality voting discarding correct answers already present (L55). Under equalized thinking budgets a single agent matched or beat six multi-agent architectures at every budget above 100 tokens; multi-agent configurations won only at very low budgets or under severe context corruption (L56).

**Extra rounds and extra effort do not buy quality by default.** Higher reasoning effort did not improve accuracy in 21 of 36 runs (L23); reasoning-heavy orchestrators underperformed lighter ones (L02); early-exit probes can cost more than they save (L60).

**Evaluator exploitation is the default failure of every self-improvement loop examined.** DGM's own appendix reports an agent that reached a perfect "solve hallucination" score by deleting the logging tokens the detector relied on, more often when the checking functions were visible (L34); iterative code optimization produced 73.8% proxy-only gains rising with optimization steps (L64); every frontier agent saturated visible tests while held-out composed suites exposed a gap growing with task length (L65). The one system reporting reduced hacking (L39) achieved it only by scoring on private signals the inner agent never observed.

**Harness evolution has not beaten matched-budget test-time scaling on held-out tasks.** With matched budgets and disjoint splits, evolution gave +0.0 on a held-out average and lost to parallel sampling (69.7 vs 82.0 pass@1) (L42); trivial sampling baselines matched or beat code-evolution pipelines in three domains and evolved scaffolds dropped more than ten points from validation to test (L52); HarnessCompass's own motivation is that prior methods overfit the evolution tasks (L41).

**Learned rules and procedures generalize unreliably and are not revised on contradiction.** No method showed a statistically significant rule-revision effect (L44); every automatic memory method showed negative transfer in at least one setting (L45); an intermediate harness collapsed from 43/80 to 6/80 solved before recovering (L43); wholesale rewrites cause context collapse (L07).

**Same-family judging is correlated judging.** Models agree on wrong answers about 60% of the time when both err and judges credit the shared wrong answer; larger, more accurate models are more correlated even across providers (L61); judges favour related generators (L62); self-recognition predicts self-preference (L63).

**RSI loops are expensive and inconsistently costed.** About USD 22,000 and two weeks per DGM run (L34); about USD 13,000 per method for GEA (L36); eight days for AIDE² with dollars unstated (L39); millions of tokens per harness update (L43); DARWIN produced statistically insignificant gains in a toy setting (L49).

**Strong recursive self-improvement is undemonstrated.** The most recent position paper lists sustained acceleration under independent evaluation, meta-improvement of the selection mechanism, and persistent gains on novel tasks as not shown, and records that 14% of Gödel Agent trials ended below the initial policy (L48); the system closest to meta-improvement keeps parent selection and evaluation fixed by hand and saturates (L38); few works test whether gains persist when evaluation conditions change (L47).

**Agentic scientific discovery lacks diversity and belief revision.** Inter-provider hypothesis similarity of 0.64–0.69 approaches within-model similarity; querying ten models raised the effective hypothesis count only from 2.9 to 3.6 (L30); only 38% of surveyed AI-scientist works provide reproducibility or novelty artifacts (L31); agents reach 38.78% on multi-paper tool-augmented reasoning against 83.5% for a human expert (L32).

## 6. When is single-pass screening enough, and when is adaptive continuation worthwhile?

**Single pass is enough, and is the right baseline, when** the evidence needed is already in context and no new external input will arrive within the round. A second pass without external information is statistically re-sampling (L57), intrinsic self-correction often degrades answers (L58), homogeneous exchange costs 2.1–3.4 times the tokens for no gain (L55), and a single agent given the whole budget is at least as good as any split above very small budgets (L56). For per-candidate assessment this means one well-contexted pass per candidate per criterion, producing the assessment record with `pass`, `fail`, `unknown_missing`, `unknown_conflicted`, `inapplicable`, or `not_assessed`; `unknown` is a legitimate output of that pass.

**Continuation is worthwhile only when something external changes the information state.** Five triggers are supported, and each must reference an artifact in the Open-Question Register (C8):

1. New external evidence or a tool result (the only condition in which revision reliably helped: L57's oracle bit; L07 and L39's execution feedback). The trigger references a new Evidence Ledger claim or Tool Broker result.
2. An unresolved evidence conflict between two ledger claims marked as conflicting; this is a missing-information case, not a disagreement between agents about the same claims.
3. A consequential missing measurement: a criterion verdict is `unknown_missing` for a candidate whose leaderboard state would change if the value were known.
4. A questionable exclusion: a hard-rule `fail` supported by a single, low-independence source.
5. A targeted computation expected to change a decision: a declared tool request with its question and the decision it could flip (L42's regime where evolution helped was granular, verifiable feedback).

Two commonly used triggers are **not** supported: an agent's self-reported low confidence (L59 relies on it, but L61 and L57 show confidence and agreement are inflated by correlated error and conformity) and "the agents disagree" without an identified referent (L55's conformity and consensus collapse). Disagreement between cross-family reviewers about a specific ledger claim is admissible as trigger 2.

**Stopping criteria the evidence supports** (C15 termination reasons): `sufficient_support` (the candidate sweep is complete and no open question carries an expected decision change; definition revised on 13 October 2026, design §6.3 and §6.6, which also add `round_limit_reached` for the normal end of a single-pass run); `no_actionable_step` (every open critique lacks an external referent); `diminishing_information_gain` (two consecutive rounds produce identical leaderboard snapshots with no new ledger claims; L60's answer-stability rule is the closest analogue, and its warning that aggressive stopping loses correct answers maps to leaving incomplete candidates `unassessed` or `provisional`, never silently resolved); `repeated_failure` (the same tool request fails after its retry budget; the item goes to the Human Review Queue, not to another round); `budget_exhausted` (yields `unresolved`, never acceptance; L39 warns that a limit-forced result entering an archive poisons later comparisons). An "information-gain threshold" stop is not computable here because ΔI needs a finite answer space and ground truth (L57); only observable Round Progress verdicts are used.

**Comparison design** (from L42, L52, L56, L22–L24): single-pass, fixed-round, and adaptive configurations run on the same task context, candidate universe, evidence snapshot, tool set, and evaluator; budgets are matched on tokens, tool calls, and human-review minutes; the adaptive policy's cost of deciding whether to continue is counted; a budget-rich single pass (P0+) is required or "adaptivity helped" is unfalsifiable; results are reported as probability of improvement with confidence intervals across repeats. A longitudinal leaderboard is maintained by evidence-refresh runs (trigger 1) and never requires an individual run to be multi-round.

## 10. Access gaps and what this survey is not

- **Blocked or missing primary material.** OpenReview blocked both the forum and the PDF of "Reward Hacking in Self-Improving Code Agents" (L64); its numbers come from the iclr.cc abstract and search snippets and should be re-verified. The secondary study behind L30's "68% of traces ignored evidence / 26% refutation-driven belief revision" figures (Ríos-García et al., 2026) was not located; those figures are indicative only. AstaBench HTML returned HTTP 404 for both versions; the PDF was read for pages 1–20 and appendices beyond page 20 were not read. The τ²-bench repository was listed but not fetched. The Hyperagents cost appendix (D.2) was not extractable. The GEA official code could not be located; the AIDE² outer loop is not public. The 97-page survey (L47) was read in sections only. The "Dynamic Agent Skills" and "Towards Persistent Growth" surveys, EvoFlow, and EvoAgentX were seen at title or snippet level only and are not in the tables.
- **Venue status.** Verified for MAST, ACE, ReasoningBank, GEPA, MASS, AstaBench, τ²-bench, BAMAS, the position paper (L30), DGM, HGM, GEA (workshop), Preference Leakage, Correlated Errors, Gödel Agent, the TMLR memory survey, and the RSI workshop poster. Everything else is "preprint, not verified", including ABC, HAL, Kosmos, and most of the 2026 self-evolution and evaluation papers. Many of the strongest 2026 results are workshop papers or preprints a few weeks old; recent publication is not a reason to adopt.
- **Reading mediation.** Full-text reads were performed through a tool-mediated summarizer with section-targeted passes; quoted statistics were not independently re-derived from tables. Where a number depends on a single pass, the design treats it as indicative and the evaluation plan does not import it as a threshold.
- **Scope.** No biomedical self-improvement literature was searched. The negative evidence on debate used 7–8B models and text-only multi-hop reasoning without tools (L55, L56); the memory benchmarks used web, coding, finance, trading, and enterprise tasks, several explicitly excluding scientific research (L10); the harness-evolution audits concern coding and games. Whether drug prioritization, which is long-horizon and tool-heavy but lacks granular verifiable feedback, falls on the side where evolution helps cannot be settled from this literature; no evidence was found that strategy evolution helps in domains whose feedback is expert judgment rather than execution.
- **What this survey is not.** It is not an exhaustive or systematic review; it is a dated, depth-labelled investigation adequate to decide which mechanisms to adopt, defer, or avoid in this architecture, and to state which claims remain unestablished (Section 7 and `evaluation_plan.md`).
